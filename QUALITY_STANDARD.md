# Portfolio quality standard

The user asked for materially stronger work after reviewing the initial intermediate exercises. Prioritize depth, defensible evidence and a useful analytical workflow over project counts or impressive titles.

- Use a substantive, specific industry decision and data at an appropriate level of detail. Prefer original public sources, documented permissions and pinned, auditable inputs.
- Separate observed facts from synthetic data, scenario assumptions and modeled outcomes. Never present assumed intervention effects as measured savings.
- Define the information cutoff, comparison baseline, acceptance criteria and limitations before assessing results. Preserve holdout periods and appropriate dependence/clustering.
- Implement data contracts, source lineage and reconciliation. Preserve discrepancies and quarantine affected inputs when appropriate; do not force data or models to pass.
- Provide a usable workflow, meaningful SQL and decision-ready outputs where they serve the problem. Extra dashboards, algorithms or test counts alone do not make a project advanced.
- Verify methods with hand-worked cases, independent reconciliation and reproducibility; distinguish code checks from data/model acceptance gates. State which visual, functional and external validations were actually performed.
- Keep an issue register, candid assessment and personal understanding. Explain what the work demonstrates and what evidence is still missing for real adoption.
- Do not claim equivalence to 8–10 years of professional experience, production certification or guaranteed recruiter interest.
- Retain the two-project goal and existing schedule, but report fewer completed if the quality bar cannot be met. Do not publish shallow filler to meet a count.

Reference implementation: flagships/utility-reliability-due-diligence. Its unresolved issues are part of its honest assessment, not requirements to reproduce the same industry or method.
