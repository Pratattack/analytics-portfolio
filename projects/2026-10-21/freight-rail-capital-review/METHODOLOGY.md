# Freight rail capital review design

## Decision and measurable objective

A rail capital-allocation/treasury committee must distinguish accounting cash-return capacity from evidence for cutting reinvestment. Objective: reconcile annual operating cash, cash PP&E expenditure and shareholder payments; identify negative residuals, calculate the cash-flow change needed to cover the observed mix, and expose asset-denominator sensitivity before a current budget review. The zero-change historical baseline is explicit. No optimization substitutes for safety or maintenance requirements.

## Cohort, scope and vintage

Three US listed rail parents, UNP, CSX and NSC, selected to keep USD/GAAP/entity scope narrow. This excludes Canadian carriers, BNSF and the rest of freight logistics; it is not an industry census. Use original-current-period annual 10-K facts, selected from the earliest filing within 120 days of the period end, with annual flows and productive assets from the same accession. Require explicit CFO, revenue, operating income, capex, dividends and buybacks. Missing facts are excluded, not zeroed; continuing-operation-only CFO and equity-repurchase totals are not silently treated as the specified consolidated/common series. The 32 admitted annual observations are an unbalanced history; the latest three observations share FY2025. Historical cohort totals must not be interpreted as a balanced panel.

UNP changes its productive-asset definition to include finance-lease right-of-use assets in 2024. Retain this separate basis, and suppress returns spanning the definition boundary. The 2025 return uses 2024 and 2025 on the same basis. Peer returns are not perfectly harmonized asset measures. Net book values and acquisitions cause further denominator distortions. Original latest filing cells independently reconcile all 24 selected current source facts. Later-disclosure differences are kept in the revision audit. Current API snapshots do not reconstruct immutable original API vintages.

## Exact cash accounting and reverse stress

Residual = CFO − cash PP&E expenditure − common dividends − common repurchases. Potential buyback capacity = max(0, CFO − assumed capex − observed dividends), before acquisitions, debt, cash floors, leases, other financing and other uses. A negative residual shows that these specified uses exceed CFO. It does **not** identify which expenditure was financed with debt, because cash is fungible and balance-sheet cash and other flows are outside this subtotal.

The CFO change at which residual is zero equals (capex + dividends + buybacks)/CFO − 1. A positive result means more CFO is required; a negative result is the decline that exhausts the baseline surplus. Scenario cash flows apply this exact equation to CFO changes of −40/−30/−20/−10/0/+10% and capex scales of 80/100/120/150%. These 72 conditional cases are a grid, not a distribution or forecast. Three-year coverage sums consecutive annual flows to reveal persistence without assuming a one-year shortfall is structural. Sensitivity analysis does not estimate probabilities or a causal capital-return response.

## Productive-asset return challenge

Proxy after-tax fixed-asset return = GAAP operating income × (1−assumed tax) / average beginning/ending same-basis productive assets. Compare net and gross book assets, tax rates 21/25/30%, and assumed hurdles 6/8/10/12%. The proxy surplus = assumed after-tax operating income − assumed hurdle × average assets. Required EBIT to hurdle = hurdle × average assets/(1−tax). This is not corporate ROIC: working capital, goodwill, debt, corporate cash, replacement cost and other invested-capital adjustments are absent. Gross versus net comparisons challenge apparent productivity driven by asset age; they are not a preferred adjusted valuation.

## Decision gate

The actual adoption gate stays unmet: FY2025 is dated; current commitments, capital guidance, liquidity, merger terms, maintenance/growth split and safety constraints are missing. GAAP income includes charges/gains, including NSC's incident/disposal-related effects; no causal interpretation is available. Reconcile current plans and operational constraints with treasury before discussing discretionary distributions; do not infer that a low proxy return justifies maintenance cuts. No realized savings, funding deficit forecast, current credit conclusion or buyback authorization is claimed.
