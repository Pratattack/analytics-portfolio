# Executive decision brief — freight rail capital and cash returns

**Decision:** review discretionary cash returns alongside reinvestment commitments with treasury. Do not infer that this screen authorizes buybacks, proves debt-funded repurchases or justifies reducing rail maintenance.

## Coherent 2025 evidence

Across UNP, CSX and NSC, operating cash is $18.264B, reported cash PP&E capex is $8.897B and common dividends plus repurchases are $10.032B. The specified residual is **−$665M**. Two issuers have a negative subtotal. Cash is fungible; balance-sheet cash and other sources/uses are outside this equation, so the residual does not identify the financing of any individual payment.

The unbalanced historical panel contains 32 admitted issuer-years and 256 selected numeric source operands. All 24 latest annual source facts independently match numeric inline-XBRL cells from original filings. Thirteen missing/coherence exclusions remain visible, as do later revision differences. Latest comparisons use the same FY2025 rather than mixing a stale UNP year with current peers.

## Conditional stress and accounting sensitivity

Sixty-two of 72 scenario cells have a negative residual after the specified cash uses. They vary CFO from −40% to +10% and capex from 80% to 150% of the annual base, not scenario probabilities. The minimum UI capex ratio is a calculator limit, not evidence that such a budget is safe.

Operating-income returns on average net and gross productive assets vary materially with the denominator and assumed tax/hurdle. They are fixed-asset accounting proxies, not issuer ROIC, replacement-cost returns or incremental project IRRs. UNP's finance-lease-inclusive reporting boundary is explicit; cross-basis adjacent-year returns are suppressed. Gains, incident charges and acquisitions are not normalized away.

## Recommended sequence and tradeoffs

1. Reconcile the 2026 treasury plan, available liquidity, committed capital and transaction/merger obligations before using the dated annual base.
2. Separate mandatory maintenance, safety and committed growth projects with operations; obtain a validated minimum investment schedule.
3. Challenge discretionary distribution flexibility against the cash plan and downside cases. Retain acquisitions, debt and other uses in the actual plan.
4. Evaluate individual projects using their own risk-adjusted cash flows; do not use a low book-asset proxy as a maintenance-cut rule.

The analysis makes the reinvestment/distribution tradeoff inspectable. It does not establish a current funding deficit, safe capex reduction or causal effect of buybacks on operations.
