Who funds private credit, and what do the balances mean?

The note reports $1,458 billion of private loans to U.S. businesses, while the nonfinancial-business debt table identifies $1,310 billion, or 5.62% of its debt denominator. The difference reflects scope. Domestic bank funding is $349 billion in the published subtotal; it must not be added to fund lending as if it were a separate loan to the same end borrower.

What changed in 2026?

The September 2026 Financial Accounts release separately identified private-credit vehicles and instruments. The new visibility includes reclassification of existing positions. It should not be presented as new borrowing that happened during the release month.

Methods and limitations

HTML tables are parsed from a hashed official publication snapshot. A scope ledger separates U.S./foreign vehicles and broad-business/nonfinancial-business borrowers. SQL independently reconciles rounded equity identities. Scenario equity = reported equity minus the assumed marked asset loss; assets fall by the same amount. The source rounding residual is preserved.

The observations are 2025 Q4 balances published in October 2026, not current live holdings. Aggregate populations conceal individual fund dispersion. Private loans may include some syndicated loans; foreign positions use estimation assumptions. Missing cells remain null. CLO equity is not separately reported, so CLOs are excluded from equity-buffer comparisons. No redemption, liquidity runway, default probability, recovery forecast, mark-to-market timing or institution-level contagion is inferred.
