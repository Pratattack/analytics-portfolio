# What would make a fuel-retail investment work?

A feasibility memo's sales estimates added up. Its investment conclusion still depended on costs, margins, financing and site approvals that were not established in the supplied evidence.

I built a scenario-based underwriting lab to make those dependencies visible. It separates lottery ticket proceeds from retailer commission, translates category estimates into cash available for debt, models staged construction draws and an opening sales ramp, and compares core scope with a hypothetical car wash.

Under one explicit illustration, the average sales case produces 0.98x stabilized debt coverage and 0.61x in the first twelve operating months, below a selected 1.25x screen. Those are conditional model results, not actual project performance. Visitors can change the assumptions, identify break-even sales and export their calculations.

The source audit also finds four inconsistent classifications across a 31-risk register. Eight site-specific evidence gates remain unverified even when a financial scenario passes. The project keeps that distinction visible: a plausible spreadsheet is not a completed investment case.

Original reports are not distributed. Identifying details are omitted. Report estimates, verified external references and hypothetical assumptions are labeled separately. Python and SQL support reproducibility, and the browser calculation is independently reconciled against the Python model.
