The reference finding

Using the report’s average sales estimates, an assumed $2,780,000 eligible core project budget, 70% debt funding, 8.5% fixed annual interest and 20-year amortization, stabilized coverage is 0.98x. Cash available is $16,504 per month against $16,888 of debt service. The six-month sales ramp lowers opening-year coverage to 0.61x.

These results do not establish that the actual development is unviable. No actual budget, loan terms, operating history or complete underlying feasibility data were provided. Eight site-specific evidence gates remain unresolved.

Development funding and delay

The reference path requires $1,134,770 of sponsor funding through opening and reaches $1,223,540 through 36 operating months. Opening inventory and working capital exclude the operating loss reserve. Additional operating deficits require $88,770 over that horizon.

Which inputs deserve the next evidence dollar?

First obtain a civil site plan, access evidence, a scope-matched contractor budget and lender terms. Clarify sales categories and obtain documented gross margins and staffing coverage. Those inputs can change the screen more directly than polishing the original risk colors.

Methods and limitations

What the model does

Start with a report sales case. Replace lottery face-value receipts with the assumed commission, apply category gross margins and card costs, add net fuel contribution, and deduct loaded staffing, fixed operating costs and maintenance reserves. This yields pre-tax cash available for debt service, not accounting net income.

Monthly debt service uses a fixed-rate amortizing loan on the selected debt share of acquisition, soft costs and construction including fully spent contingency. Development interest is paid by the sponsor on average monthly drawn balances. Acquisition and soft costs are paid at closing; hard costs draw evenly after any added permit delay. Pre-opening carry and loan fees are separate sponsor cash uses. No financing is assumed for these uses or working capital.

Operating sales ramp linearly while staffing and fixed costs start in full. Opening working capital means inventory and other working-capital investment, excluding the loss reserve. It is funded once. Peak sponsor funding adds the maximum cumulative operating deficit through 36 operating months; it is horizon-dependent, not lifetime funding needs. Monthly debt amortization continues during the operating period.

The financing-capacity ceiling solves for the eligible budget consistent with the selected stabilized coverage ratio at the assumed leverage and term. It is not fair value, an acquisition offer or a first-year coverage guarantee. Break-even fuel volume holds nonfuel contribution fixed. The all-sales threshold changes all categories together with fixed staffing. Both are algebraic scenarios, not demand forecasts.

What is excluded

Income taxes, depreciation, tax shields, terminal sale proceeds, appreciation, refinancing, prepayment, balloons, future working-capital changes and itemized fuel inventory float are excluded. No lender covenant, actual return, NPV or IRR is asserted. Category overlap, sales taxes, franchise fees and supplier/payment arrangements need source clarification or explicit cost allowances. A sales increase does not automatically prove unchanged staffing is achievable.

Risk scores remain subjective ordinal ratings. They are not failure probabilities, expected losses or simulation distributions. A corrected label does not validate the underlying rating, and a planned mitigation does not prove a lower residual risk.

Reproduce the findings

Run python analyze.py, python build_review.py, the Python tests and Node parity checks from the project directory. Analysis is offline and uses sanitized extracts and explicit scenario inputs. The original documents are not bundled.
