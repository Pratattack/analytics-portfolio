Credit Spread Recession Signal Audit

Does adding credit spreads improve a yield-curve baseline? Revised monthly history, 1970–2024; evaluation origins 2008–2023.

Decision brief

Across 192 test months, candidate Brier loss is 0.1676, versus 0.1750 for the curve model and 0.1487 for the unconditional baseline. Lower is better.

Retain the simple unconditional benchmark. The credit model improves on the curve model here but fails to beat the unconditional benchmark. Its block interval crosses zero and only two distinct positive target runs occur. Withhold predictive promotion.

How the decision is bounded

Candidate minus curve Brier difference: -0.00736; 24-month circular-block interval [-0.06712, 0.03537]. At the calibration-selected threshold of 0.15, the candidate produces 120 false alerts and 2 missed positive months. These are overlapping months, not independent event counts.

NBER turning points are dated retrospectively. A twelve-month label gap does not recreate actual announcement availability. FRED history is revised, not ALFRED vintage data. BAA/10-year spreads retain a maturity mismatch and incorporate liquidity and risk-premium effects. The positive-target runs and monthly outcomes are dependent; block bands describe historical resampling, not guaranteed future coverage. False-alert costs are assumptions.
