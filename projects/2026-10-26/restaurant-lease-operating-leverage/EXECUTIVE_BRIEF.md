# Executive brief: separate rent accounting, cash and response capacity

**Recommendation:** use the scenario frontier to prioritize operational diligence; do not treat it as an approved turnaround plan or cash forecast.

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---:|
| Retained panel | 32 issuer-years; six issuers |
| Latest original filings / source cells audited | 6 / 24 |
| Selected FY2025 operating lease expense | $1.089B |
| Selected FY2025 operating lease payments | $1.099B |
| Reference stress cases | 216 |
| Infeasible within assumed caps | 32 |
| Feasible only with both levers | 33 |
| Additional cap/recovery sensitivities | 72 |

The accounting control matters: EBIT already includes rent. A stress that deducts base lease expense again understates earnings. Expense and lease cash also differ, so an earnings review cannot be relabeled a liquidity review. The six fiscal years do not share identical dates.

The workbench combines an assumed sales decline, lost-sales contribution margin, incremental lease escalation and EBIT floor. It finds the minimum assumed response resources using capped cost savings and recovered sales. Each lever has execution risks: service quality may decline, sales recovery may require discounts, and contractual rent may not flex. The resource weights are scenarios, not observed implementation costs. The 32 infeasible cases preserve the need for more evidence or a different plan.

Request store-level contribution margins and lease reset schedules first, then challenge response caps, one-off costs and demand effects. Current cash, debt, covenant definitions and maintenance investment would be required for a treasury recommendation.

## Personal understanding

I used restaurant disclosures to understand how fixed operating commitments interact with falling sales. I first reconciled revenue, operating earnings, lease expense and lease payments to source filings, because similar accounting labels can describe different things. I then measured the earnings gap under explicit assumptions while avoiding a second deduction of rent already included in operating earnings.

The optimization is conditional: it tells me the least assumed response resource needed if cost savings and sales recovery are available within the caps. It does not tell me those actions are realistic. In an interview I would discuss the 32 infeasible cases and explain why the next step is operational diligence, not claiming that the model found actual corporate savings.
