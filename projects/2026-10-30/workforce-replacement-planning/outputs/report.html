Growth is only part of the hiring budget.

At the trailing 12-month private-sector separation-rate average of 3.56% per month, a hypothetical employer growing from 1,000 to 1,100 staff needs 548 hires, not 100. The difference is replacement demand under the stated recurrence.

Observed evidence: seasonally adjusted U.S. JOLTS data through August 2026, retrieved October 30. Scenario: 1,000 starting staff, 10% growth, 12 months, constant monthly hiring and an industry-rate proxy. This is not an employer forecast.

The business decision

Before approving 100 additional roles, estimate the recruiting capacity needed to replace exits as well. A monthly hiring limit can prevent the headcount target from being reached even when the budget covers the nominal new positions.

Method and reconciliation

Headcount evolves as N[t+1] = N[t] × (1 − s) + h. Constant h is solved analytically to reach the target; replacement hires equal total hires minus net headcount change. Zero-turnover and zero-hiring boundaries are explicit. SQL independently calculates trailing-rate means; 126 bulk-file observations reconcile exactly to separate release-table cells across levels, rates and three months.

Job openings are a stock at month-end; hires and separations are flows during the month. Openings are retained for context, never divided into hires to claim a vacancy-filling probability.

Limitations and validation detail

National industry estimates are not firm-specific turnover probabilities. Total separations include quits, layoffs and other exits. Rates use BLS employment denominators and are rounded. The recurrence instead applies the chosen rate to beginning-month modeled staff, an explicit planning approximation. Same-month hires and exits are not modeled separately. Staffing, growth, capacity and cost inputs are hypothetical. Revised and concurrently seasonally adjusted history is not a real-time vintage backtest. Overlapping three-month targets are dependent, so no significance test or forecast interval is claimed. Latest August estimates are preliminary. Current staffing decisions need company-level evidence.

Low and high cases are the observed minimum and maximum of the last 12 monthly industry rates, not confidence limits. A target below natural attrition’s zero-hire outcome requires additional separations, which this tool flags rather than assuming negative hires.
