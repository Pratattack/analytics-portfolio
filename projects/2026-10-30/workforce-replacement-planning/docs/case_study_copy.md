# How much hiring does growth really require?

100 net new roles can require about 548 hires in the reference scenario. Replacement demand changes the recruiting budget and can expose a capacity shortfall.

## Finding
At the 3.56% trailing mean monthly private-sector separation rate, a hypothetical employer growing from 1,000 to 1,100 staff needs about 548 hires over twelve months. Roughly 448 replace exits. Forecast methods selected on validation beat the last-month baseline in four of seven series, tie in one and do worse in two. This does not establish production forecast quality.

## Decision use
A budget for 100 new roles may leave most recruiting work unaccounted for. Test replacement demand and monthly hiring capacity together, then replace the industry proxy with company-level exit evidence before approving a plan.

## Methods
Analytical headcount recurrence separates replacement hires from net expansion. Use a trailing 12-month mean separation rate as a clearly labeled industry proxy. Independently reconcile inputs in SQL and 126 source-table cells. Chronologically select among four prespecified forecasting baselines using 2023-2024 validation and evaluate 2025-2026 held-out target windows.

## Limits
National rates are not employer-level exit probabilities. The model applies the proxy to beginning-month staff, which differs from the official denominator. Targets, costs and capacity are scenarios. Revised and concurrently adjusted data prevent a real-time backtest claim. Target windows and industry aggregates overlap. No causal conclusion, predictive interval or actual employer result is claimed.
