What the optimization does

A linear program allocates purchase dollars to three bill tenors and cash. It maximizes terminal spendable cash, subject to capital conservation, nonnegative holdings and a minimum reserve after every payment. The baseline holds all money in zero-yield cash. A sensitivity grid changes reserves, early stress payments and upfront proportional fees.

For discount quote d and days T, price per dollar of face = 1 − dT/360. Maturity factor per allocated dollar = 1 / [(1 − dT/360)(1 + fee/10,000)]. Coupon-equivalent values are retained in raw data but never substituted into this formula.

Decision implication

Ask how much cash is available before each liability falls due. A higher-yielding long bill cannot fund a day-14 payment without an early sale, which this model disallows. Change the payment assumptions before interpreting the allocation.

Limitations and implementation requirements

Indicative secondary-market bid quotations are not executable offers. Fixed 28-, 56- and 91-day tenors stand in for actual security settlement and maturity dates. No reinvestment, early sale, bid-ask spread, taxes, lot sizes, holiday adjustment or credit facility is modeled. Cash earns zero in the baseline. Actual cash forecasts, offers and security identifiers are required before implementation. Reserve and payments are hypothetical; this is not a forecast of a real company’s cash.
