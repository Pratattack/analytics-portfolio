# Airline delay intervention screen

A fixed ten-slot investigation screen captures 43.4% of eligible positive peer excess in Jan–Aug 2026; the raw-burden baseline captures 33.3%. Retrospective stability gate: PASS; prospective deployment gate: NOT MET.

## Decision

Use the shortlist for structured operational investigation only. Do not purchase a delay-reduction program from this evidence. The screen adjusts each carrier-airport-month against other operating carriers at the same airport and month, excluding itself. It freezes the candidate universe and priorities using Jan–Aug 2024; checks Jan–Aug 2025; diagnoses Jan–Aug 2026 without retuning. Both policies share ten slots and a two-per-airport cap.

The capture difference is 10.04 percentage points; its exploratory two-month-block interval is [5.94, 13.07]. This is concentration of an accounting benchmark, not intervention lift. The denominator is only the discovery-eligible pool still observed, not the entire US network.

## My understanding and interview defense

A busy airport can generate many delay minutes without having unusually poor performance. The comparison uses another-carrier benchmark at that airport in that month, so it asks where a carrier departs from a common local environment. Its signed excess can be negative. Late-aircraft, NAS and weather minutes are not relabeled as controllable carrier minutes.

The comparison still lacks schedule, fleet and route adjustment, and eight diagnostic months do not establish generalizable superiority. Historical data are today's revised snapshot, so the time split is a retrospective stability study, not a point-in-time backtest. I should show a source row, independently compute its excluded-self peer rate, explain the capacity constraint and explain why a pilot is needed.
