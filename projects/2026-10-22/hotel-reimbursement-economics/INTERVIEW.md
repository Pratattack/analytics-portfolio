# Personal understanding and interview defense

I investigated why a hotel operator's reported revenue and operating margin can be misleading for a program-budget discussion. Reimbursements can be a very large part of revenue even though their revenue-minus-expense balance is small or negative. I constructed an exact bridge that removes reimbursement revenue from the denominator and its net balance from income. Across the three issuers, that changes the H1 2026 margin ordering, but it still does not isolate pure fee profit because owned hotels, co-brand activity and other costs remain.

The budget work asks what recovery growth would be needed to fund extra program costs while preserving an assumed income floor. It is a conditional accrual model, not a forecast of cash receipts. I should explain why the −$326.224M H1 reimbursement balance cannot be called a funding deficit, why annual/half-year/quarter observations cannot be added together, and which contract and forward-budget evidence would be needed before authorizing spending.

## Five grounded interview questions

1. **What is the key accounting identity?** B=R−RR and P=O−RR+RE, so O=P+(RR−RE). This exactly separates the net reimbursement balance, without claiming that P is pure fee profit.
2. **Why not just use issuer adjusted EBITDA?** The question concerns a specific revenue/expense boundary. Other issuer adjustments differ; this bridge preserves those remaining items and is auditable rather than treating all adjusted measures as equivalent.
3. **How did you avoid double counting?** Every observation retains annual/half/quarter type. Annual SQL summaries exclude overlapping periods, H1 current analysis uses H1 only, and Q1 is explicitly derived from same-filing H1 minus Q2.
4. **How was the constraint independently checked?** Separate Decimal arithmetic recomputed each scenario and bisection solved the income-floor equation without reusing the analytic required-growth formula, for all 216 cases.
5. **What prevents adoption?** Unknown contractual recoverability, settlement timing, forward core budgets and service obligations. A negative accrual balance is not observed cash burn, and small selected historical samples do not establish persistent losses.
