# Honest portfolio assessment

This version is materially deeper than the original ten-district resilience simulation. It demonstrates real data acquisition, an audited panel with schema changes and lineage, SQL accounting, model comparison, clustered uncertainty, balanced-panel sensitivity, release quarantine, explicit promotion gates and a local/offline decision workflow.

It is best described as a **substantive advanced portfolio research prototype**. The depth lies in evidence handling and decision judgment, not in calling a median model sophisticated. It is not equivalent to 8–10 years of professional experience, a commissioned utility engagement, engineering validation or a deployed production platform.

The strongest interview discussion is why the newer release is not automatically used, why the apparent predictive improvement is inconclusive, and why assumed interruption reductions cannot be marketed as measured savings. The project contains reproducible evidence for those decisions.

The largest gaps are unresolved 2025 source-component differences, limited independent test years, publication-vintage reconstruction, omitted physical/hazard covariates, unobserved intervention effects and absent stakeholder validation. Browser acceptance is now tracked by its own reproducible CI job; see BROWSER_ACCEPTANCE.md and the actual result for the reviewed commit. Browser success does not resolve the research and adoption gaps.

My understanding: I can trace a result back to an official workbook row, show how the reporting method and denominator affect comparison, explain why utility-level clustering matters, and demonstrate a conditional business case without pretending its assumptions were observed. I should also be able to explain what would cause the model or data release to fail an acceptance gate, and what evidence would change that decision.
